Applies to `manuscript/SGWB_PE_AI_draft_2026-10-31.pdf` (the 2026-10-31 ING-Net draft). Its LaTeX source is not on the

## 0. The two things a referee would catch first

1. **One amplitude axis.** Table II and Table III of the draft (log10 Ω ≈ −7.3 to −7.6) are still the paper-v1 numbers.
   They were produced with the v1 calibration constant S_code = √(2 ∫ S_gw/P_n df), which carries an extra factor
   √(2 · 980 Hz) ≈ 44.3 relative to the Eq. (11) σ1 = √(∫ S_gw/P_n df / 980 Hz) (rev1 change R2 in
   `docs/source_tables/CHANGES_rev1.md`; σ1 = 37.63 for the O3a reference stretch, S_code = 1666). For the same injected
   variance, log10 Ω_new = log10 Ω_old + 2 log10(44.3) = log10 Ω_old + 3.29, which is why Table V (−4.4 to −4.9) sits
   3.3 dex above the old Table II. The v1 numbers also come from the v1 signal model (one burst per segment of length
   0.2 ξT) and the v1 whitening, so they are not simply shifted copies of the new ones; they are replaced, not converted.
   Replace Tables II and III with `docs/tables/table2_15stretch.md` and `table3_null_ul.md`; Fig. 7 with
   `docs/tables/fig7_sensitivity_curves.pdf`; Fig. 8 with the O4a null distributions on the new axis (ING-Net −4.68,
   Trad −4.45, Δ_paired = +0.23 dex, Table III row 04A). With one axis, the comparison requested by the referee reads:
   at ξ = 10⁻² the per-segment ING-Net limits are log10 Ω = −4.73 to −5.25 (15 stretches; mean −4.95), i.e. 3.0–3.5
   orders of magnitude (mean 3.3) above the LVK O3 isotropic limit Ω_GW ≤ 5.8 × 10⁻⁹ (log10 = −8.24).
2. **Training-time glitch augmentation is not described.** The primary configuration behind Table II
   (`rev1_glitchaug`, `rev1x_glitchaug`) trains every network with synthetic single-detector glitches added to 10 % of
   the training segments (sine-Gaussian or blip, SNR 5–3000, `glitchaug_b.py`); the evaluation noise is untouched. The
   draft never says so. Add to Sec. III B, after "early stopping with a patience of 30 epochs":
   > "During training only, a synthetic single-detector transient (a sine-Gaussian or a blip with matched-filter SNR
   > between 5 and 3000) is added to one detector in 10 % of the simulated segments, with the parameters θ of the
   > segment unchanged. This teaches the estimator that excess kurtosis in a single channel is not evidence for a
   > background; without it the loudest real glitches in the evaluation noise set the 99.7 % threshold and the
   > ING-Net limit on the reference stretches is 0.4–0.5 dex shallower (Table V, which uses networks trained without
   > augmentation, against Table II)."
   and in Sec. VI A (coherency veto) say that the separation of the glitch locus from the signal diagonal is
   partly learned from this augmentation rather than emergent, and that Table V (un-augmented networks plus an
   explicit coincidence veto) shows the two routes give the same Δ.

**Two findings about the product of posteriors (state in Sec. V F; they bear on option (b) as a method):**

1. *Coverage degrades with N.* Injections from the prior into N real segments, product posterior on a 0.02 dex grid, 200
   trials per run, 45 ING / 41 Trad runs: coverage of the central 50 % (90 %) interval is 0.49 (0.90) at N = 1, 0.38 (0.78)
   at N = 10, 0.28 (0.47) at N = 100 for ING-Net; 0.51 (0.89), 0.46 (0.82), 0.30 (0.52) for Trad. KS distance of the PIT values
   exceeds the 95 % critical value in 9/45 runs at N = 1, 35/45 at N = 10, 45/45 at N = 100 (Trad 0/41, 25/41, 40/41). On
   synthetic whitened noise the single-segment coverage is nominal for every run (KS above critical 0/9, 0/6), so the
   residual per-segment miscalibration is a real-noise effect. The product is usable as an empirically thresholded detection
   statistic, not as a calibrated N-segment posterior. Replace the draft sentence "coverage remains within the 2σ band at
   every N" (now done in the drafts file).
2. *As a detection statistic the product is shallower than the linear stack of the same posteriors*, on real noise by
   +0.10/+0.09 dex (ING/Trad) at N = 30, +0.15/+0.10 at N = 100, +0.16/+0.12 at N = 246; on synthetic noise by 0.2–0.7 dex at
   N ≥ 10³, where the noise-only product statistic no longer moves with the data (0.3 % threshold within 0.01–0.04 dex of
   the median at N = 10³): the shared systematic shape of the learned posteriors, raised to the power N, dominates. Those
   synthetic product-rule rows are therefore not usable and are greyed in the figure. Grid resolution is not the cause: on
   03A seed 1400 the limits on a 0.02 dex grid equal those on the 0.1 dex grid at N = 30, 100, 246 (`real/results/gridcheck_03A_seed1400.json`).

Wording for the manuscript: the real-noise closure confirms the synthetic analysis; the product-of-posteriors construction
is a detection statistic whose calibration does not survive multiplication and which is dominated by outlier segments on
real noise; a calibrated multi-segment posterior needs a calibration correction before multiplication or a network
conditioned on stacked summaries (future work).
